The short version

  • We read distance, duration and activity type from your workouts. Nothing else. Not heart rate, not calories.
  • We never write anything to Apple Health.
  • While you are tracking a session, we use your location even when the screen is off — that is how a run keeps counting in your pocket. iOS shows the blue indicator while it happens, and it stops when the session does. When no session is running, the only thing that reads your location is the button that centres the globe on you, at the moment you press it.
  • Nothing about you is public. What you publish is seen by the people who follow you (or by the close friends you pick), and by the people in the same run as you when you put something into that run. A time capsule is seen by whoever reaches the same stop.
  • Nothing is used in a book or a product unless you switch that on, per card and per contribution. Off by default.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app, at any time, and it is gone.

Creating an account

You can create an account in three ways, and we store the same profile whichever you choose:

  • Sign in with Apple. Apple gives us a stable identifier for you and — depending on what you choose in Apple's own dialog — either your email address or one of Apple's private relay addresses. If Apple's own sign-in sheet fails on your phone, you can use email instead.
  • Sign in with Google. Google gives us a stable identifier for your Google account and its email address.
  • Email and password. Your password travels over an encrypted connection to our sign-in service, which keeps only a one-way hash of it. Nobody at WeStride can read your password.

We ask for your date of birth to confirm you are 13 or older — the day, not just the year, because age changes on a birthday. We store it for that one purpose; it is never shown in the app, to you or to anyone else, and it is used for nothing else.

Your profile has a display name, a username you choose, a home country (suggested from your phone's region, and yours to change), and — if you add them — a profile photo and a banner. Who can see the photo is described below.

We ask you to agree explicitly to three texts, and we record each agreement: which version of the text you saw, when, and which account it was. The three are the onboarding consent shown together with this policy, the content rules (before your first time-capsule contribution), and the product consent (the first time you switch it on). Those records exist so we can prove what you agreed to, and so a change to a text means asking you again rather than assuming. They are deleted together with your account.

What we collect, and why

Workouts. From Apple Health, and only if you allow it: start and end time, time zone, duration, distance and activity type, plus the identifiers Apple gives each workout so we can recognise one we have already imported and never count it twice. The Health permission we ask for covers your workouts and the distance samples behind them (walking and running, cycling, and wheelchair distance) — nothing else. We deliberately do not request heart rate, calories or any other health metric, so we could not read them even if we wanted to. We also do not take elevation from Health. With your permission, iOS wakes the app about once an hour to import new workouts even when you have not opened it. If you record a session with WeStride's own GPS tracker, we keep for that session its pace, its kilometre splits and its elevation gain.

Location. WeStride uses your location for two things, and nothing else.

While a session is being tracked, we use it to measure how far you move. That continues when your screen is off or the app is in the background, so a run keeps counting in your pocket — iOS shows the blue location indicator the whole time it is happening, and it stops the moment you end the session. A session in which you did not move at all is discarded; under a hundred metres the app asks whether you want to keep it.

The other use is the button on the globe that centres the map on you. It takes one position at the moment you press it, and only then.

When no session is running, the app does not read your location — not for the weather, not for the home-screen widgets, not for anything else.

The path you walked. When WeStride tracks a session with its own GPS, we keep the path that session took, so you can look at your own run afterwards and choose to put it on a card you share. It is yours: only you can read it, and it is deleted with your account like everything else here.

Two things are true about it that we want you to be able to check.

*Nothing is published until you publish it.* The path stays private to your account. When you finish a session the app offers you a card, and you decide what that card is: the route you ran, drawn whole on a map — or how far you have come toward your goal, which shows the goal's route and your progress along it, and does not show where you were. Choosing the second one is how you share a session without sharing a location. Nothing is posted, and nothing leaves the app, until you tap Publish or Share.

If you choose the route card, it shows the route as you ran it, from start to finish. We do not crop it, shorten it or move it — a picture of your run that is not your run would be worse than no picture. That means the route card shows where you started, so choose the goal card instead when you would rather not show that.

WeStride on Apple Watch. If you use the watch app, it tracks a run with the watch's own GPS and keeps the same things the phone's tracker keeps: the path, pace, kilometre splits and elevation gain. Two things are different on the wrist. The watch saves the run to Apple Health as a workout — distance, duration and activity type, nothing else: no heart rate, no calories — so it shows in your fitness history; that workout is marked as WeStride's own, and the phone's Health import skips it, so a run is never counted twice. And the watch signs in as you once, through your iPhone, and keeps its own sign-in from then on; signing out on the phone signs the watch out too. A run the watch cannot upload yet (no signal) stays in WeStride on the watch until it can. The Apple Health copy syncs across your own Apple devices the way Health always does; WeStride sends it nowhere else.

*Sessions imported from Apple Health have no path.* Health gives us distance, duration and activity type; it gives us no route, and we do not ask for one.

*The name of the place.* To say where a GPS session was («Rossabøskogen»), the app sends the middle point of that session's path to the place-name service named below and keeps the name it answers. The name is shown to you on the session, and on a card only if you publish that card.

A session also carries a coarse position — rounded to about 10 km (one tenth of a degree), never where you actually were — so the app can say roughly where a session happened and fetch the weather for it. This is covered by the consent text you agree to during onboarding.

«Running now». While you track a session, the app tells our server every twenty seconds (every ten when somebody else in the run is out too) that you are out, with how far you have come and your average pace so far. It does not send your position. In a friend run and in a race — a friend race or a community race — the other members can see that you are running now, with your name and face, and your pace if the run shows pace and you share yours; the app refreshes that picture every fifteen seconds. In a community run that is not a race nobody is shown by name or face — the others see only how many are out and the group's average pace. Two switches in your profile control this: *Show when I am out running* hides you entirely — nobody sees you as active and nobody is told when you start — and *Share my pace* turned off means your pace is neither shown with your name nor counted into the run's average. In a race, and in a run with a meeting point, the others still see how far along the route you are, because that is what a race and a meeting are. Members of a run can turn on *Live alerts* to get a notification when someone in that run starts a session. We stop showing you as active three minutes after the last signal.

Applause while you run. People in your run can tap applause while you are out. We store who applauded whom, in which run and when, so the tracker can show it to you and send you a notification; the notification carries the name of the person who applauded.

Goals and progress. The goals you create (name, start and end place, and the route computed once at creation), and a ledger of how many kilometres from which session went to which goal. The ledger is append-only: a correction is recorded as a new entry rather than by rewriting the old one, because the count has to be auditable. In a race we also record when you crossed the finish and each stage, and your placing; in a friend run the other members see your kilometres in it.

Planned runs. You can plan a session for a time and place — on its own or in a run: the time, a meeting point you choose on the map, an optional note, who you invited, and each person's answer. The plan is seen by you and the people you invited, nobody else; the app can remind the people who said yes shortly before it starts. Plans and races appear in your calendar in the app and, if you add it, in the calendar widget.

Journal. In a run with the journal turned on you can write entries as you go: text, an optional photo, how far along the route you were, and a place name. The place name is computed from the point on the run's route at that distance — the place-name service receives that route point, not your device location. A journal in a solo run is private to you; in a friend run the other members read each other's entries.

Time capsule. At a stop on a run you can leave a contribution: a text, a photo or a short video. It is seen by the other members of the run who reach the same stop — this year, and in later years' editions of the same run when they get there. You can withdraw it at any time. What is checked before others see it is described under *Blocking and reporting*.

Achievements, banners, streaks and cards. Which milestones you have reached, which stages you have won, which banners you have earned, your current streak (shown to your followers only if you leave *Share my streak* on), and — only when you actively publish a card — a feed entry other people can applaud and comment on. When you publish a card you choose its audience: your followers, or only the close friends you have picked. You can also share it into a run you are in; the members of that run then see it too, followers or not.

Following. WeStride works like the follow model you know from other social apps. We store whom you follow, who follows you, follow requests you have sent or received, the people you have marked as close friends, and whether your profile is private. With a private profile, new followers have to be approved by you.

Comments and reactions. Comments you write on cards, replies to other people's comments (a reply can name the person it answers with their username), hearts you give comments, and your applause on cards and posts.

Community, News and polls. WeStride's staff post in the Community and News channels: text, a picture, or a short video. On a community post you can comment and applaud, and vote in a poll. Your vote is stored against your account so you can change it; other people, including staff, only ever see the totals, never who voted for what. News posts take neither comments nor applause.

Notifications. We keep a list of what happened that concerns you — a follow request, applause, a comment, a reply, a stage won, someone starting a session in your run, a plan you were invited to — with who did it. If you allow notifications, we also store the push token iOS gives your device and the language you chose in the app, so the notification can be written in it.

Home-screen widgets. If you add WeStride's widgets, the app keeps a copy of what they show in a protected store on your phone that only WeStride and its widgets can read: your numbers, your calendar, the latest journal words in your runs (yours and the other members'), and the weather. The weather in the widget is fetched for the coarse position of your most recent session — the widget never reads where your phone is. The copy is erased when you sign out.

Product consent and original photos. Each card you publish and each capsule contribution has a switch, off by default, that lets WeStride use it in the books and products made for that run — for example the shared book of a community run or the yearbook — together with your first name. When you turn it on for a card we also keep the original photo behind the card, not only the rendered card, so it can be printed properly. We record the consent: which text you saw, and when. You can turn it off again at any time; from then on it is not used further, but what is already printed cannot be recalled. A book you order of your own run needs none of this — it is yours.

Weather. For each session we store the weather at its start — a weather code, temperature and precipitation — fetched once from Open-Meteo using the session's coarse position and start time. The widget's weather uses the same coarse position, at most every half hour.

Membership tier and rewards. Whether your account is on the free tier or has unlocked more with a code, and which rewards you have earned. A code you redeem is recorded against your account so it cannot be used twice.

Camera, microphone and photo library. The app asks for these only when you choose to take or pick a photo or a clip for a card, a time capsule or, for staff, a post. Only what you take or pick is used; your library is never browsed or uploaded.

Small things the app remembers on your phone. The language you chose for the app (a setting of the phone, kept across sign-outs), and for each channel the moment you last looked at it, so the app can count what is new since — those marks are cleared when you sign out.

Blocking and reporting someone

Both create a record about another person, so they belong here rather than only in the app.

When you block someone, we store who blocked whom and when. That record is what makes the block work: it hides their comments, their applause, their shared cards, their journal entries and their time-capsule contributions from you, and yours from them, on every surface. A block is not announced to the person blocked. You can undo it, and undoing it deletes the record. A block changes nothing in a poll's totals — subtracting a blocked person's vote would let a reader work out how they voted.

When you report a comment, a card, a journal entry or a time-capsule contribution, we store what you reported, the reason you chose, any text you wrote, who you are, and when. We need all of it to act on the report and to see patterns across reports. The person you reported is not told who reported them.

A time-capsule contribution reported by three people is hidden until WeStride's staff have looked at it; a card or a journal entry is hidden the same way. When staff decide to hide, remove or show something again, we log the decision: which contribution, what changed, who decided, and when.

Reports and moderation logs are kept even after the reported content is gone, because a record of why something was removed is the point of having them.

How long we keep it

For as long as you have an account, and until you delete it — with these exceptions:

  • Twelve months after a run has ended, the posts made in it are deleted and the cards shared into it stop being shared there — the card itself stays with its author. We run this clean-up by hand for now, and a run without an end date keeps its feed.
  • A time-capsule contribution stays with the run for as long as the run exists, because next year's runners are meant to find it — unless you withdraw it.
  • Reports and moderation logs are kept as documentation.
  • When staff delete content in bulk (for example old comments on News), we keep a count of what was deleted and when — numbers only, no names.

Some of what you contributed does not belong only to you: kilometres you put into a shared pot are part of a total other people can see, and a comment you wrote sits in someone else's conversation. Kilometres you put into a shared pot (a friend run or a community run) stay in it, without your name, after you delete your account — as one anonymised entry per run, shown as «Former participants», so the total the other members saw does not change. Kilometres on a race are yours alone and are deleted.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app (Profile → Privacy). When you do, we delete your profile, your workouts and their paths, your kilometre ledger entries (see above for the anonymised kilometres a shared pot keeps), your goals, your achievements and banners, your race results, your shared cards, your journal entries, your time-capsule contributions, the comments, hearts and applause you gave, your poll votes, your plans and answers to plans, whom you follow and who follows you, your blocks, your notifications and push token, and your consent records — together with the files behind them: your profile photo and banner, and the photos and videos you uploaded, including those you left in other people's runs.

Goals you created that other people are also in are not deleted — they are handed over to the member who has been in them longest, so deleting your account does not delete other people's goals along with it.

A book or product that has already been printed with your consent cannot be recalled by deleting the account. A notification that has already been sent to somebody's phone cannot be recalled either.

Deleting your WeStride account does not touch your data in Apple Health. We never wrote anything there.

Your rights

If you are in the EU or EEA you have the right to see the data we hold about you, to correct it, to have it deleted, to restrict or object to how we use it, and to receive it in a portable format. Deletion is built into the app; for anything else, write to us at the address above.

You also have the right to complain to a supervisory authority. In Norway that is Datatilsynet.

Children

WeStride is for users aged 13 and over. We ask for your date of birth during sign-up and do not create a WeStride profile for anyone who has not yet turned 13; if you cancel at that step, the sign-in you started is deleted as well, as soon as the app can reach our server (until then it only signs you out, and cancelling the same step on your next sign-in deletes it). From 13 you create the account yourself in the app. When WeStride is used at an organised event, the organiser collects consent from a parent or guardian for participants aged 13 to 17, outside the app, before they take part. The product consent described above can be given from the age of 13.

Your workouts say something about your health. We treat them with the care the law asks for health data — as a precaution, whether or not the law would count a distance as such: we collect the least we can, we never read heart rate or calories, and we never use any of it for advertising.

Changes to this policy

If we change how we handle your data in a way that matters, we will ask for your consent again in the app rather than only updating this page. That is why the consent record stores which version of the text you saw.
